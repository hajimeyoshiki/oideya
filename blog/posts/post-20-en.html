Solaniwa Onsen OSAKA BAY TOWER Review [2026]

June 10, 2026Oideya
For visitors who want a proper hot spring experience without leaving the city, Solaniwa Onsen OSAKA BAY TOWER is a hot spring theme park in Osaka's bay area that delivers exactly that. Natural hot spring open-air baths, a garden-view bath overlooking a rooftop Japanese garden, and a yukata-clad atmosphere that feels genuinely like a ryokan getaway — all reachable within the city. On our visit, our kids got completely absorbed in the festival corner, and we noticed plenty of international visitors changing into yukata and taking photos throughout the grounds — this is a place that works equally well for families and guests from abroad. What is Solaniwa Onsen OSAKA BAY TOWER

Opened in 2019, Solaniwa Onsen OSAKA BAY TOWER is one of Kansai's largest hot spring theme parks, built around the concept of "a hot spring village of contentment and joy." The interior blends old-fashioned Japanese townscapes with a modern aesthetic — this isn't just a bath, it's closer to a self-contained mini-trip where you change into a yukata and wander between baths, food stalls, and festival-style corners.

The total floor area exceeds 5,000 tsubo (about 16,500 sqm), with a rooftop Japanese garden of roughly 1,000 tsubo (about 3,300 sqm) called "Tenku Teien" (Sky Garden). The "Niwami-yu" garden-view bath, which lets you soak while looking out over this garden, is one of the facility's standout features.

Getting there from Oideya

From Oideya Guest House (Kanzakigawa Station), the route to Solaniwa Onsen runs via Umeda. Take the Hankyu Kobe Line from Kanzakigawa to Osaka-Umeda, then transfer to the JR Osaka Loop Line to Bentencho. The Umeda-to-Bentencho leg takes about 10 minutes by train, so the full journey from Oideya including the transfer runs roughly 30–40 minutes.

Bentencho Station is directly connected to the Solaniwa Onsen building, so there's almost no walking or wayfinding once you exit the station — a genuine convenience if it's raining.

When to go

A good pattern: spend the day sightseeing in Umeda or Dotonbori, then head to Bentencho in the late afternoon or evening. You can soak until close, letting the onsen wash away a full day of walking before heading back to Oideya.

Pricing — a day-of-week system worth knowing

Solaniwa Onsen uses a variable pricing structure by day of the week, which is somewhat unusual among Japanese bathhouses. Weekdays are cheapest, rising in stages through Friday, weekends, and peak days. If your schedule is flexible, knowing this structure can save a meaningful amount.

Pricing changes by date

Because pricing is fine-tuned by weekday, Fridays, weekends, and peak dates, check the exact price for your intended date on the official website beforehand. A weekday visit can save close to ¥1,000 compared to a weekend one.

The baths — nine varieties to work through

The biggest draw of Solaniwa Onsen is the sheer range of baths on offer. Both the men's and women's areas feature a distinct selection built around a genuine natural hot spring source.

Natural hot spring open-air bath

The facility's flagship bath, drawing on its own spring source. A convincing hot-spring atmosphere, right in the middle of the city.

Garden-view bath (Niwami-yu)

Overlooks the rooftop 1,000-tsubo Japanese garden. Soaking while watching the seasonal garden change is unique to this facility.

Carbonated spring bath

Fine carbonation bubbles surround you in this bath, said to promote circulation. Good for a long, slow soak.

Rotating seasonal bath

The bath changes periodically, giving repeat visitors something new each time.

Fully private open-air baths

Popular with couples and families wanting privacy. A private view of the bay, without sharing the space.

Sauna & body scrub

A proper sauna is included for those who want to complete the loop, with a body scrub option available too.

What it was actually like

Looking out over the Sky Garden from the garden-view bath, it was hard to believe this was only a short journey from central Osaka — the space felt genuinely quiet and open. Visiting in the late afternoon into evening was particularly striking: the garden's lighting and the rising steam overlapped into a distinctive scene. Time spent soaking in the open-air bath, followed by a stretch at the foot bath overlooking the garden, felt like a real indulgence.

Beyond the baths

Solaniwa Onsen isn't purely a bathing facility. The yukata-based dress code and Japanese aesthetic extend into several other areas worth building time into your visit for.

  • Foot bath: A wide foot bath spans the short side of the garden — a relaxing way to take in the view without a full soak.
  • Rock bath sauna (ganbanyoku): A separate heated-stone sauna area for those who want a proper sweat.
  • Dining: Izakaya-style food and traditional sweets are available, including a popular brûlée-dango soft drink and craft beer.
  • Festival corner: A yukata-friendly festival-style space, popular for photos.
  • Seasonal events: Summer festivals and other seasonal programming appear periodically.

Practical notes before you go

Amenities are covered

Towels, bath towels, soap, body wash, shampoo, and hair dryers are all provided free of charge. Arriving empty-handed is fine.

Visiting with children

Under 4 bathes free. Note that groups of under-16s only are permitted until 19:00, and under-18s only until 22:00 — worth knowing if you're planning a visit with a group of minors. On our own visit, having the festival corner available made a real difference — the kids stayed happily occupied for a long stretch, which meant the adults got a proper, unhurried soak as well.

If you're driving

The facility has parking for 1,100 vehicles, with a 2-hour (¥800 value) discount for bathing guests. If you're coming from Oideya by train, though, this is unlikely to be relevant.

Avoiding the crowds

Weekends and peak dates draw the biggest crowds. A weekday morning, right after the 11:00 opening, gives you the best chance of soaking in a quiet bath and enjoying the garden without a wait. Late afternoon into evening picks up as people arrive after work.

Combining it with a stay at Oideya

If you're staying at Oideya Guest House, Solaniwa Onsen works well as the closing act to a full day of Osaka sightseeing.

Sample day plan

Morning to afternoon: sightsee the classics — Umeda, Dotonbori, Osaka Castle → Late afternoon: take the JR Loop Line via Umeda to Bentencho and Solaniwa Onsen → Evening: soak in the open-air and garden-view baths, then a light meal at the dining area → Return: back to Umeda, then Kanzakigawa and Oideya.

Washing away a full day of walking with a real hot spring, before returning to a quiet place to sleep — for anyone wanting to add "something more" to an Osaka trip, Solaniwa Onsen is worth the detour.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Solaniwa Onsen OSAKA BAY TOWER cost?
Admission varies by day. Weekdays are ¥2,310 for adults, Fridays ¥2,640, weekends ¥3,080, peak days ¥3,300, and New Year period ¥3,630 (plus a ¥150 bathing tax). Children (age 4 to elementary school) are ¥1,320, and under 4 is free.
What are Solaniwa Onsen's opening hours?
The facility is open 11:00–23:00, with the main baths usable until 22:30. Last entry is generally around 22:00, so arriving earlier gives you more time to enjoy everything.
How do I get to Solaniwa Onsen OSAKA BAY TOWER?
It's directly connected to Bentencho Station on the JR Osaka Loop Line and Osaka Metro Chuo Line, about 10 minutes by train from Osaka/Umeda Station. From Oideya (Kanzakigawa Station), the route goes via Umeda, with a total journey of roughly 30–40 minutes.
What kinds of baths does Solaniwa Onsen have?
There are nine types of baths for men and women, including a natural hot spring open-air bath, a garden-view bath overlooking the rooftop Japanese garden, a carbonated spring, and rotating seasonal baths, plus a sauna and body scrub service. Fully private open-air baths are also available.
Is Solaniwa Onsen good for families with children?
Yes. There's a festival corner with classic Japanese games like shateki shooting galleries and yo-yo scooping, and on our own visit our kids stayed completely absorbed there for a long time. Alternating between the baths and the festival corner means the whole family, including adults wanting a proper soak, comes away satisfied. The yukata-changing experience is also popular across a wide range of visitors, including international guests.
