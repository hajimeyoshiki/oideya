Wi-Fi, SIM & Currency Exchange
A Complete Arrival Guide for Osaka [2026]

September 1, 2026Oideya
The moment you step off the plane at Kansai International Airport, two questions come up almost immediately: how do I get online, and how do I get cash? This guide walks through the Wi-Fi, SIM, and currency exchange options at Kansai Airport, with the pros and cons of each, so you're not figuring it out on the spot. Worth reading before you head toward Oideya.

① Getting online — Wi-Fi router or eSIM

Kansai International Airport does have free public Wi-Fi, but for anything beyond basic browsing, it's worth arranging your own connection. There are three main options.

📶

Option 1: Rent a Wi-Fi router

Terminals 1 and 2 at Kansai Airport have counters for several rental companies, including Global WiFi and Imotonowifi. Booking online in advance means you can collect your device the moment you land — the smoothest option available. Since one router can be shared across a group, this works particularly well for families and groups travelling together.

Returns are usually as simple as dropping the unit into a return box at the airport, so you don't need to plan around your departure time. Daily rates vary by company and plan, so it's worth comparing before booking.

📱

Option 2: Buy a physical SIM card

The arrivals area at Kansai Airport has SIM card vending machines, and prepaid SIMs are also available at the Wi-Fi rental counters. If your phone is SIM-free (or carrier-unlocked) and supports the right bands, you can simply swap the card in and be online within minutes.

No need to carry an extra device, which makes this a good option for solo travellers who want to keep things light. One thing to note: while your Japan SIM is in, you won't be able to receive calls or texts on your home number.

💳

Option 3: eSIM (downloaded in advance)

If your device supports it, you can download an eSIM before you travel and activate it the moment you land in Japan. There's no counter to queue at and no vending machine to figure out — switch off airplane mode and you're online. Handle it before you leave, and there's literally nothing to do once you arrive.

Book ahead if you can

Both Wi-Fi routers and SIM cards are best booked online in advance. Same-day sign-ups work if stock is available, but during busy periods devices and cards can sell out. Once your travel dates are confirmed, it's worth booking early.

② Getting cash — exchange offices vs. ATMs

Japan still relies on cash more than many visitors expect. Local shotengai shops and smaller restaurants in particular don't always accept card or contactless payment. Having a reasonable amount of yen on hand from the start makes the first day or two much less stressful.

Using the airport exchange counters

Terminal 1 at Kansai Airport has several currency exchange counters and bank desks, plus a 24-hour automated exchange machine. The convenience of sorting this out the moment you land is real, but it's worth knowing that airport exchange rates tend to run somewhat worse than exchange offices in the city.

On exchange rates

Rates and the margin added on top of the mid-market rate vary between counters. A reasonable approach: exchange only what you need for the first day or two at the airport, then top up later using a city exchange office or a convenience store ATM once you've had time to compare.

Withdrawing cash at a convenience store ATM

ATMs at Japanese convenience stores — particularly Seven Bank ATMs at 7-Eleven — accept a wide range of internationally issued cards. Visa, Mastercard, UnionPay, American Express, JCB, Discover, and Diners Club cards can all be used to withdraw Japanese yen directly.

  • Withdrawal limit: ¥100,000 per transaction for foreign-issued cards (¥30,000 for magnetic stripe cards)
  • Fees: ATM usage fees vary by card brand (your card issuer may also charge a separate fee on their end)
  • Availability: Seven Bank ATMs are generally accessible 365 days a year
  • Language support: Screen instructions and printed receipts are available in multiple languages, so the process is easy to follow

7-Eleven stores are everywhere in central Osaka, and there are several within reach of Oideya in the Kanzakigawa area too — worth remembering as your go-to if you're running low on cash mid-trip.

③ Wi-Fi at Oideya during your stay

📡

The in-house Wi-Fi

Oideya Guest House has a fixed in-house Wi-Fi connection. The network name (SSID) and password are clearly posted inside the room, so you can connect as soon as you check in. Even if your airport Wi-Fi or SIM arrangements didn't quite work out, or your phone battery is running low, arriving at Oideya means a working internet connection is immediately available.

Free Wi-Fi around Kanzakigawa and Umeda

Beyond the guesthouse itself, free Wi-Fi is increasingly available around Osaka, including near Oideya. The main options:

  • Train stations: Major stations including Kanzakigawa, Juso, and Osaka-Umeda often offer free Wi-Fi provided by the railway operator (a one-time email registration is sometimes required)
  • Convenience stores: Major chains including 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, and Lawson generally offer in-store free Wi-Fi
  • Cafés and chain restaurants: Starbucks, McDonald's, and similar chains commonly provide free Wi-Fi
  • Shopping complexes: Large Umeda facilities like Grand Front Osaka and LUCUA also offer in-building free Wi-Fi (see our Umeda area guide)
  • City-provided Wi-Fi: Services like "Osaka Free Wi-Fi" cover parts of the city as well
A note on public Wi-Fi

Public Wi-Fi is open to anyone, which means security is worth thinking about. Avoid online banking or anything involving sensitive personal data on open networks, and consider a VPN if you'll be doing anything that requires it.

④ Practical tips

🏪

When in doubt, use a convenience store ATM — Seven Bank especially

Between exchange counters, ATMs, and card payments, the single most reliable option when you actually need cash on the spot in Osaka is a convenience store ATM — specifically Seven Bank at 7-Eleven. Three reasons: ① stores are everywhere, including around Oideya in Kanzakigawa, and most are open 24 hours, so late nights and early mornings aren't a problem; ② the range of accepted foreign card brands is wide, letting you withdraw yen directly; ③ the interface supports multiple languages, so there's little guesswork involved.

Hunting around the city for an exchange office is often slower and less certain than just finding the nearest 7-Eleven. Make convenience store ATMs your default option.

🚨

Have your emergency contacts ready

Worth noting down before you travel, just in case:

  • Police: 110 — for crimes, accidents, or theft. English-language support is generally available even if the initial call is in Japanese
  • Fire & ambulance: 119 — for fires, sudden illness, or injury
  • Interpretation services: Multilingual tourist information and emergency support lines (such as the JNTO Tourist Information Center) are worth having noted before you arrive
  • Your accommodation: If something comes up during your stay at Oideya, the contact details provided at booking are the first point of call

Having Wi-Fi or a SIM sorted also makes it much easier to share your location or use a translation app in the moment. Staying connected isn't just about convenience — it's a genuine safety net if something goes wrong.

⑤ A practical strategy that combines everything

Rather than thinking about Wi-Fi, SIM, and currency separately, it helps to plan them together around your travel style. Here's a sensible sequence for after you land.

🎯

A model arrival flow

1. Arrange an eSIM or Wi-Fi router in advance — handling this online before you fly keeps waiting time on arrival to a minimum.

2. Get a small amount of cash in the arrivals hall — enough for the first day or two, using either an exchange counter or an ATM. There's no need to exchange a large sum all at once at the airport.

3. Top up as needed in central Osaka or near your accommodation — use convenience store ATMs, including those near Oideya, as your ongoing source of cash throughout the trip.

Mix in card payments too

Major tourist areas and chain stores across Osaka widely accept credit cards and IC-based contactless payment. Treat cash as a backup rather than your primary method, and lean on cards and IC cards for everyday spending — it cuts down on how much exchanging you need to do at all.
